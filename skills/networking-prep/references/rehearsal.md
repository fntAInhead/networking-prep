# Conversation rehearsal

## Establish the exercise

Resolve the intended person and event from the conversation and saved records. Read the user's profile, event goal, and relevant brief. If no brief exists, build a small supported background from available evidence or offer a generic professional rehearsal clearly labeled as such. Do not guess a person's identity to start practice.

Introduce the exercise as a **hypothetical practice simulation**. State the grounded background and any invented conditions briefly. Describe observed writing tendencies as uncertain evidence about public communication, not a complete personality model.

Use the user's intended opener or let them begin. If they ask the simulated person to start, give one plausible opening and wait. Default to approximately four to six exchanges or the user's desired duration. Text exchange counts approximate a short encounter and do not measure actual speaking time.

## Persona boundaries

Separate three ingredients in the brief or exercise header:

- Sourced professional background, with evidence references.
- Tentative public communication observations and their limits.
- Fictional practice conditions, such as busy, receptive, brief answers, a skeptical follow-up, or a group interruption.

When style evidence is sparse, use a neutral professional voice. Avoid imitating a distinctive voice closely or claiming to be the actual person. Use `Simulated [name]` for in-character replies.

Respond plausibly from the documented background. A hypothetical opinion, preference, invitation, or commitment must remain visibly within the simulation. Avoid inventing new employers, achievements, personal relationships, sensitive details, or job openings. When asked something beyond the evidence, give a general hypothetical response or pause to clarify that it is unknown rather than manufacturing biography.

## Interaction loop

Run one response at a time and wait for the user's next message. Listen to their actual wording; build on it instead of reading a fixed script or supplying their side of the conversation.

Default to an uninterrupted realistic encounter, then feedback. Support these natural requests at any point:

| User request | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Pause / explain / coach me | Exit character, answer or coach, and wait for the user to resume |
| Coach every turn | Give concise coaching after each exchange, separated from simulated speech |
| Restart / try that again | Reset the exercise or retry the last exchange; keep the same supported background |
| Harder / easier / make them busy | Adjust fictional conditions and explicitly label the change |
| End / debrief | Stop character speech and give feedback |
| Show an example conversation | Write a labeled fictional example; do not mistake it for an actual practice transcript |

Available scenarios include a brief introduction, limited time, short answers, a challenging follow-up, joining a group, a topic that does not land, and closing with a next-step request. Select one appropriate condition by default; do not make every interaction adversarial. Fictional conditions do not update the person's factual record.

## Feedback and saving

Evaluate what the user actually did across specificity, listening, reciprocity, clarity, authenticity, and the next-step request. Use concrete examples from their turns. If a dimension did not arise, mark it unobserved instead of assigning a score. Do not claim to evaluate body language or spoken delivery from text alone.

Give a short account of what worked, two or three useful improvements, and concrete rewrites in the user's tone. Flag an unsupported claim or exaggerated expertise directly and offer a truthful alternative. Suggest one exchange worth retrying.

Save concise feedback under the event's practice directory with contact/event IDs, scenario, date, observations, and retry suggestions. Mark the record **simulation feedback**. Save no full transcript unless requested, and do not copy generated exchanges into the research or real interaction records. Keep coaching suggestions out of established profile facts unless the user adopts them.

A simulated agreement to meet, introduce someone, send a resume, or make a referral creates no real commitment. If the user later asks for a follow-up based only on a rehearsal, explain that no real interaction is recorded and offer a first-contact draft or ask what actually happened.
